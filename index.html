Five small steps

Every letter is made of two sounds. A short beep called a dit (·) and a long beep called a dah (–). Start with the shortest letters and work up. Tap a letter to hear it, say it out loud, then quiz yourself.

Patterns that make it stick

    Beginner tips. Say it, don't count it. A is "di-dah", B is "dah-di-di-dit". Practice 10 minutes a day. A wrong guess is fine, just replay it and try again.

    The exact timing, and how the speed lessons work

    The word PARIS drawn to scale, units 0 to 50. Speed in WPM means how many times you could send PARIS in one minute. At 20 WPM one unit lasts 60 milliseconds.

    The five timing rules

    • dit1 unit of tone
    • dah3 units of tone
    • inside a letter1 unit of silence between parts
    • between letters3 units of silence
    • between words7 units of silence

    How this course teaches

    Koch method. You start with two to four characters at full speed and add more only when you copy 90 percent correctly. You never learn a slow version you later have to unlearn.

    Farnsworth spacing. Letters play at 20 WPM, and the extra time goes into the gaps. You get time to think without the letters changing shape.

    Ears only. Patterns are hidden by default. If you picture dots and dashes, you add a lookup step that caps you around 10 WPM.

    Three habits that decide how fast you learn. Practice every day in short sessions, two 10 minute blocks beat one hour on Sunday. When you miss a letter, write a dash and move on, chasing it makes you miss the next three. Say "dit" and "dah" out loud when you hum a letter, never "dot" and "dash".

    2Build speed

    Ten lessons, forty characters

    Done the five steps? Now train your ear to catch letters at real speed. Tap each new character a few times and hum it back. Then play a drill, type what you hear, and check. Score 90 percent or better and move to the next lesson. Expect two to five days per lesson.

      • Make dahs three times your dits. Most beginners send dahs too short.

      Choosing a real key

      A straight key is the classic up and down lever, you make every dit and dah by hand. It is cheap and teaches rhythm. An iambic paddle with an electronic keyer makes the dits and dahs for you. Most operators move to a paddle once they pass 15 WPM.

      A sending routine

      Five minutes a day is enough. Send the alphabet, then numbers, then a sentence from a book. Record yourself on your phone and copy it back the next day. If you can't read your own sending, nobody else can either.

      5On the air

      Your first real contact

      Most people learn Morse to use it on ham radio. A basic CW contact, called a QSO, follows a script, so you can make one long before you can copy free conversation.

      1. Get licensed

        In the US there has been no Morse test since 2007. The entry Technician license already gives you CW on parts of the 80, 40, 15 and 10 meter bands. Find a test session through the ARRL at arrl.org. Other countries have their own exams.

      2. Listen before you transmit

        Copy the ARRL station W1AW, which sends code practice on a published schedule at several speeds. With no radio yet, tune a WebSDR receiver online at websdr.org and listen near the bottom of the 40 meter band.

      3. Learn the standard exchange

        A first contact is CQ, callsigns, signal report, name, location, and 73. Practice the lines in the QSO drill in stage 3 until you can copy them at 15 WPM overall.

        CQ CQ DE K1ABC K · K1ABC DE W7XYZ K · W7XYZ DE K1ABC UR RST 579 NAME JOHN QTH OHIO BK

      4. Join a club that holds slow sessions

        CWops runs the free CW Academy classes, the Long Island CW Club teaches online at every level, and the Straight Key Century Club hosts relaxed, slow contacts. You'll find patient operators who expect beginners.

      5. Ask for slower when you need it

        Send QRS to ask the other station to slow down. Every experienced operator was once in your seat, and almost all of them will slow down without a second thought.

      When you get stuck

      If two letters keep swapping, drill only those two for a day. If a whole lesson stalls, go back one lesson for two sessions. If everything blurs, drop overall speed by 2 WPM, never character speed.
